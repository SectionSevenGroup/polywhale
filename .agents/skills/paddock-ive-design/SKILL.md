# Paddock Ive design

Use the approved design for the product and the relevant [Apple UX/UI guidance](../apple-ux-ui/SKILL.md). The review prompts below are portfolio interpretations of Ive's published reflections on deliberate curiosity, clarity, and care. His first-person sources are [Letters to a Young Creator](https://letters.stevejobsarchive.com/jony-ive) and [Stripe Sessions 2025 transcript](https://stripe.com/sessions/2025/a-conversation-with-sir-jony-ive).

## Refine the task before the surface

- Start with what the person is trying to accomplish. Look closely at the existing interaction and test tentative ideas before committing. Treat a prototype as a way to learn whether the design resolves the underlying complexity.

- Identify the one thing a person is doing at each step. Keep the information that helps them choose, check what will happen, and recover. Remove duplicate labels and competing actions before changing the visual style.
- Make hierarchy with spacing, type, alignment, and one surface change. Remove a divider or outline when a nearby boundary or spacing already does that job. Keep visible focus, error, selection, and input affordances. Give destructive actions a clear role.
- Make pickers read as one control. A selected value needs emphasis. The picker and its buttons rarely need separate frames and separators. Keep Cancel quiet, Done discoverable, and the current value stable until confirmation.
- Refine motion as part of interaction. Follow direct manipulation without lag, give limits tactile feedback when appropriate, and settle quickly into a clear state. Use native behavior where possible. Check interruption, reversal, and Reduce Motion.
- For a sweep, inventory screen families, find repeated issues at the component that owns them, and test representative consumers in light and dark. Include the crowded state, the empty state, focus, and the smallest supported viewport. Document any unverified surface separately from changes you exercised.
