# Four complementary design lenses

Curated on 2026-10-21 for the owner's request for leading graphic, UX and UI influences. There is no objective world top-four ranking spanning these fields. These four have distinct contributions; their sources are not Apple requirements, endorsements of this skill or substitutes for user evidence. Translate principles into our own product identity rather than copying work. The review prompts below are our synthesis, not direct quotations or claims that the practitioners reviewed the product.

## Paula Scher — typography and identity

Primary evidence: [Pentagram profile](https://www.pentagram.com/about/paula-scher) describes her typographic work and integrated identities, including The Public Theater and major consumer/cultural brands.

Apply the lens to recognisability: does typography establish a clear hierarchy, and does the identity remain coherent from a small header through a poster or export? Build a coordinated visual language instead of treating the logo as a sticker. In our products, preserve original marks, deliberate scale/clear space, consistent alignment and approved type roles. Expressive display type belongs where it helps identity; it must not make controls or body text harder to use. Her expressive identity work is not evidence that our interfaces should become visually loud.

## Jony Ive — integrated craft

Primary evidence: [LoveFrom's Jony Ive profile](https://www.lovefrom.com/jony) documents responsibility across hardware, user interface, packaging and architectural work; [LoveFrom](https://www.lovefrom.com/) describes a multidisciplinary collective.

Use integrated craft as an interpretive lens: do shape, material, motion, typography and interaction feel considered together? Does each detail serve the experience? Check optical alignment, transitions, edge conditions and physical interaction at actual size. Restraint should reveal the work, rather than erase useful affordances. This is our interpretation of an integrated design practice, not a sourced Ive rule about a particular font, corner radius or glass effect.

## Jakob Nielsen — learnability and error resistance

Primary evidence: [10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/), authored by Jakob Nielsen.

Review whether the interface communicates state, uses familiar concepts, supports escape/reversal, remains consistent, prevents errors, favours recognition, accommodates efficient use, avoids irrelevant clutter, helps recovery and offers useful help. Use the relevant heuristics to explain a concrete issue and its impact. Do not equate a heuristic review with observed user testing or let visual polish hide a broken journey. Consistency means consistent meanings and patterns, not identical layouts on every platform.

## Julie Zhuo — product value and understandable quality

Primary evidence: [Julie Zhuo's site](https://www.juliezhuo.com/) and her authored [How to tell if work is beautiful / Good Design](https://joulee.medium.com/the-looking-glass-how-to-tell-if-work-is-beautiful-f6cbb080d4c7). The latter describes value, ease of use and craft, and relates understandable design to familiar mental models.

Review whether the design solves a real problem for its intended people, feels understandable and is carefully executed across the whole experience. Explain why a proposed change improves the task. Prefer a familiar solution when a novel interaction adds learning without enough benefit. A beautiful isolated frame is insufficient evidence of a useful product. Seek relevant user feedback when possible; disclose when recommendations are expert judgment rather than user-validated outcomes.

## Resolve competing advice

First satisfy the user's goal and applicable accessibility requirements, then preserve the approved product identity and familiar platform behaviour. Use typography/brand expression to strengthen hierarchy, craft to refine execution, usability heuristics to reveal friction and product judgment to check value. Do not manufacture four separate redesigns or request a celebrity-style review for every button fix.
